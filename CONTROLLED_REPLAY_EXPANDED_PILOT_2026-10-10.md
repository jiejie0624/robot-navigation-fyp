# Expanded controlled-replay pilot — 2026-10-10

## Design

This is a separate controlled planner-replay experiment, not the autonomous end-to-end benchmark. At each checkpoint, repeated A* and D* Lite receive the same current cell, goal, newly revealed obstacles, and cumulative known-obstacle set. A* searches from scratch; D* Lite retains its search state and repairs it after the same map changes. A planner-independent reachability and path validator checks each result.

The replay set has 34 deterministic traces: four hand-designed cases (single blocker, sequential blockers, off-route updates, and goal cut off) and 30 seeded generated maps (10 at each requested density: 8%, 16%, and 24%). Each trace has three checkpoints and each planner ran ten recorded repetitions. The simulator ran the set on 15 × 15 and 25 × 25 grids.

Both planners returned matching outcomes and valid paths on all 1,020 paired checkpoint observations per size; both reported no route at each disconnected checkpoint. In this pilot, D* Lite expanded fewer nodes on every trace but took longer to plan on every trace. Lower search work did not mean lower browser runtime in this implementation. These times are small and specific to one browser/device run, so they do not support a general speed claim.

## Scope and interpretation

The seeded maps broaden the earlier four-trace pilot and make replay conditions deterministic. They are generated by the simulator's own grid generator and are not a representative sample of buildings. Repetitions and checkpoints are nested within traces; they are not independent maps. The run used Chrome 155 on Windows, at a 1280 × 720 viewport and 1.25 device-pixel ratio. The CSV was retained through a temporary local-only capture request in a copy of the page's export handler; the replay and planner code were unchanged. This is one browser/device session and does not measure cross-device variation.

The earlier four-handcrafted-trace pilot is documented separately in `CONTROLLED_REPLAY_PILOT_2026-10-09.md`; do not combine its measurements with this expanded pilot.

Trace-family medians are derived from per-trace summaries so repeated observations do not inflate the independent map count. Before final evidence, repeat across browser sessions/devices and consider human-designed maps or an independent testbed.
